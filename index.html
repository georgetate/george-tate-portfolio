hermes Personal Assistant
Sep 2025 to Apr 2026

Each morning before school I would spend a little time building up the architecture for this project. After configuring all the authentication, data storage, and APIs for user information, I worked as a carpenter and saved up for a MacBook. This computer is something I had been wanting for so long that I don't think I'll ever regret my purchase. The MacBook allowed me to run hermes on a local large language model (LLM) and will give me room to build future simulations, machine learning models, and 3D graphics.

It was a really cool feeling to finally build something complete and substantial that I was proud of. This project really challenged my programming skills and forced me to step into the role of mid-level architecture development. During implementation, I was also exposed to powerful tools and industry standards like OAuth2, Google Cloud, and SQL. By far the most interesting part of the project was making tool calls to the LLM in the local Llama server because it showed me a little bit of the logic behind software like Claude and ChatGPT.

My Photography Aug 2025 to May 2026

The photography style I've gravitated to is close up framing with deep shadows. It's interesting to me because it gives a sense of wonder and curiosity about the sections of the image that cannot be seen. The result is gaps being filled by personal imagination and the brain's creativity. While close ups are my favorite, they have caused me to struggle finding exciting medium and wide shots because their wider framing reveals more of the scene, which makes it harder to show only the best part of a shot.

Something strange that has happened as I have practiced more is that oftentimes little edited pictures of my surroundings will pop into my imagination while I am driving or walking. Highway overpasses, cracks in the sidewalk, and afternoon shadows are all seen as cinematic potential that can be brought to life by combining an interesting angle, color scheme, and lighting. I've really enjoyed this side effect of photography because it helps me uncover the latent beauty and intrigue in surroundings that previously bored or depressed me.

Honda Christmas Special Restoration Jul to Dec 2025

This is a collectible Honda mini-bike that I restored with a friend from church. The restoration entailed cleaning the carburetor, some other engine work, and extensive detailing. Easily the most involved part of the process was removing all the rust from the inside of the gas tank. Because of how fun it is to ride, it was totally worth the effort though!

This mini-bike was originally my Grandpa's. I got it from my Grandma after it sat in the shed for decades. After some internal debate I planned on auctioning it to a collector in order to fund future projects, but as I was nearing the listing process my Grandma passed. Very quickly, I realized that I would rather keep it in remembrance of both her and my Grandpa. After abandoning my plan of selling it, I now consider it a family heirloom that I'll share with my own kids one day.

Soccer Jersey Prom-posal Apr 2025

During my senior year of high school I was determined to do the best prom proposal ever. I painted a custom soccer jersey and got it signed by everybody on the team. Luckily, the girl I was asking happened to be gone the day my friend on the team collected the signatures. Then I got some of my friends to wear shirts that spelled out "PROM!?" after one of her soccer games. The two best parts about it were the stadium lights and how my friends were involved. It definitely makes for a fun story and cool memory.

As for the shirt art itself, I was hoping to paint some sort of complex masterpiece. Unfortunately after a few tests, I quickly realized how difficult it is to paint details on textile. Initially, I was disappointed that I had to settle for something simpler because I knew the result wouldn't live up to my expectations. But after a little while the design started to grow on me. Now I'm even a little surprised by how much I like it.

From Scratch Coffee Table Jan to Mar 2025

This project was a bit of a learning experience because I had minimal woodworking experience. It was also particularly time consuming on account of my lack of quality wood or a workshop. My Dad helped me rough cut the pieces and assemble the final product, but everything else was just hand tools in my bedroom. I used a wood worktable, some clamps, a Japanese hand saw, various sandpaper, and a set of chisels.

Building this table really tested my patience, critical thinking, and resourcefulness, which is why it's special to me. It's evidence that I can cope with suboptimal materials, minimal tooling, and inexperience to produce something interesting and high quality.
